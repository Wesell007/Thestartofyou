## 1. C074 — Tummy time → `/first-year/development/baby-development-in-the-first-year`

**Original Phase 31 proposal:** standalone tummy time article, First Year, P1, LOW_RISK_GENERAL.

**Current owner:** the First Year development article, with tummy time already named across `firstYearMonthData.ts` and `firstYearPhaseData.ts`.

**Why a new page is no longer appropriate:** the intent is one practical technique with a small keyword cluster, already threaded through the structured month and phase architecture. A separate URL would repeat that material and compete with the development owner for the same queries. No duplicate tummy-time URL.

**Exact expansion required:** one section in the development article covering what tummy time is, that it can start from birth in short bursts on a parent's chest and build up as the baby tolerates it, that it is always awake and supervised and never a sleep position, what to try when a baby dislikes it, and how it supports head control and rolling. The month pages that already mention tummy time link to the expanded section rather than restating it.

**Review classification:** LOW_RISK_GENERAL, with the awake-and-supervised wording treated as safety copy that must not be softened.

## 2. C063 — Sleep training methods → `/first-year/sleep/helping-your-baby-settle`

**Original Phase 31 proposal:** sleep training methods article, First Year, P1, WELLBEING.

**Current owner:** `helping-your-baby-settle`, alongside `newborn-sleep-expectations` and the unpublished 32B draft on sudden sleep changes.

**Why a new page is no longer appropriate:** the demand is for named methods, most of them American in framing. A dedicated page would sit directly on top of the settling owner, overlap the 32B sleep-regressions draft, and pull the brand towards prescriptive programmes it does not endorse. Cannibalisation risk HIGH. No competing sleep-training page.

**Exact expansion required:** one section in the settling article that names the approaches parents encounter, explains plainly what they have in common, states that UK guidance does not endorse one named method over another, sets a floor that gradual and responsive approaches suit most families, notes that nothing overrides safe sleep, and points readers to their health visitor rather than to a programme. Overlap with the 32B draft is handled by keeping regression triggers in that draft and method framing here.

**Review classification:** NEEDS_HUMAN_EDITORIAL_REVIEW.

## 5. C029 — Subchorionic haematoma → `/articles/bleeding-in-early-pregnancy`

**Original Phase 31 proposal:** standalone article, Pregnancy, P2, HEALTH_REVIEW_REQUIRED.

**Current owner:** `bleeding-in-early-pregnancy`.

**Why a new page is no longer appropriate:** nearly everyone searching this has just been given the term at a scan and is frightened. A standalone page built on a named diagnosis becomes a fear-led page and competes with the bleeding owner for the same queries, when the bleeding article is already where readers land. Avoid a standalone fear-led diagnosis page.

**Exact expansion required:** a short section explaining that a haematoma is a collection of blood between the pregnancy sac and the womb lining, that it is often found at a scan, that many settle on their own and many pregnancies continue, that bleeding or brown loss can come and go while it resolves, what the maternity team may advise, and that heavy bleeding, severe or one-sided pain, or feeling faint means contacting maternity services or the early pregnancy unit straight away. No statistics beyond what a UK source supports, and no prognosis promises.

**Review classification:** HEALTH_REVIEW_REQUIRED.
