Nausea in early pregnancy: what it is, why it happens, and when it eases

Nausea in early pregnancy is caused by rapidly rising levels of hCG, the hormone your body produces after implantation. It is one of the most common symptoms of the first trimester and, while it can feel intense, it is not usually a sign that anything is wrong.

Searching "is it normal to feel this sick" at six in the morning. Wondering whether feeling fine is something to worry about. Managing daily life while feeling significantly unwell, and not knowing how long it will last. These early weeks can be relentless, and both strong nausea and very mild nausea sit within the range of normal.

Why it happens

Rising hCG levels

Human chorionic gonadotropin, the pregnancy hormone, begins increasing rapidly after implantation. hCG directly triggers the nausea centres in the brain, which is why nausea typically starts around week 5 to 6 when levels are climbing fastest.1

Rising progesterone

Progesterone slows digestion throughout the body. This slowing can lead to bloating, nausea, and a feeling of fullness, particularly on an empty stomach.

Heightened smell sensitivity

Oestrogen amplifies your sense of smell in early pregnancy. Smells that were previously neutral can trigger nausea, sometimes before you have noticed any other symptoms.

When it starts, peaks and eases

Starts
Typically around weeks 5 to 6, coinciding with the rapid rise in hCG levels.
Peaks
Often most intense between weeks 6 and 9, when hCG is climbing fastest.
Eases
For many people from weeks 12 to 14, as the placenta takes over hormone production. This varies: some feel better earlier, some experience it for longer.

The intensity of nausea is not a reliable indicator of pregnancy health. Many people with very mild or no nausea have entirely healthy pregnancies.

What is usually normal, and when to seek support

Usually normal

  • Nausea at any time of day, not just the morning
  • Symptoms that vary significantly from day to day
  • Nausea that starts, stops, and returns
  • Very mild nausea, or no nausea at all

Worth a check

  • Unable to keep any fluids down for 24 hours or more
  • Significant weight loss due to vomiting
  • Dark urine or other signs of dehydration
Contact your midwife or GP today

If you cannot keep fluids down, are losing weight, or feel your symptoms are severe.

Hyperemesis gravidarum is a recognised condition that needs medical support, and asking early is the right thing to do. If you ever feel very unwell, faint, or cannot keep anything down at all, use your local urgent care service.

Common questions

Is it normal to have nausea all day, not just in the morning?

Yes. Despite the name "morning sickness", nausea can occur at any time of day. Many people experience it more in the afternoon or evening. All-day nausea is common and does not indicate a problem.

Does no nausea mean something is wrong?

No. Many healthy pregnancies involve very little or no nausea. The absence of nausea is not a reliable indicator of pregnancy health and is not, on its own, a reason for concern.
