## Hierarchy and premium cues

1. The product name in the light display serif dominates through size and letter spacing, not weight.
2. The botanical frame stays at the corners and leaves the centre calm.
3. The subtitle is small and quiet.

The **maker logo competes with the title** on the lid: it sits in the visual centre at a heavier weight. That cue is less "premium" than the book cover, which carries no maker logo on the front.

**Restraint:** no photography, no badges, no claims, no price cues on the lid (VISUAL), which is a premium-leaning signal.

## Implications for digital

- The **brand on the product is The Start of You**, while **kidproducts is the maker brand** that appears on the packaging and the back cover. Digital needs an owner decision: whether kidproducts appears in the product at all (for example "Made by kidproducts" in the footer or about page) or only on packaging.
- The packaging's quiet centre and corner botanicals give a digital composition cue: the hero content is centred and generous, with decoration at the edges.
