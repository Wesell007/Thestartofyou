# Direction scorecard: A, B, C and the proposed synthesis

Scores are **1 (weak) to 5 (strong)**, reasoned judgements from the audit and the reference round, not measurements. Directions as defined in `journal-audit/design-principles.md` §3:

- **A, The Journal Digitised** (faithful page-for-page translation);
- **B, Editorial Keepsake** (journal typography and palette lead; illustration sparing);
- **C, Woodland Companion** (characters lead; richer watercolour fields);
- **S, the proposed synthesis** (B base + A for journal/owner/memory surfaces + C only at chapter and keepsake moments).

## 3. Does the synthesis produce one brand or three systems?

**One brand, if three rules hold.** The reference round found the seams where three systems would show:

1. **One typography and one palette everywhere.** A, B and C differ in composition and in how much art they carry, never in type or colour. If owner surfaces introduced a different type scale or stage colours, it would become three systems.
2. **Art placement is governed by surface state, not by direction.** Botanicals and characters appear only at (a) chapter openers and stage transitions, (b) keepsake and celebration moments, (c) empty and welcome states, and (d) the Journal product page. They never appear on article, hub-list, Companion, settings, account or any health-notice surface. This is the C budget, written as a rule.
3. **Keepsake devices (washes, frames, ruled lines, tape) are reserved for writing and remembering.** They appear where the user writes, records or looks back: My Week prompts, journal entries, memories, activation. They never appear on reading or informational surfaces. This is the A budget.

With these rules, a user moving from an article (B) to this week's page (A) to a new trimester (C moment) experiences one book: the same title style, the same paper, the same voice, with the page becoming more personal as it becomes more theirs. Without them, B's editorial pages and A's keepsake pages would feel like two products stitched together, and C's characters would leak into trust-critical surfaces.

**Score for the synthesis under those rules: 50/60 on criteria and 38/40 on surfaces.** The lost points versus pure B are in health trust, readability and accessibility, and all three are recoverable by the P6 rule "nothing behind text" and by state-gating the keepsake elements.

## 4. Residual risks

- **Drift risk in B:** "calm editorial" is a crowded genre. Mitigation: the dotted title rule, the spaced-capital display serif and the blush/sage washes on writing surfaces are the TSOY signatures and must survive every screen.
- **Overreach risk in A:** rotated polaroids, tape and washes behind text on mobile. Mitigation: mobile translation table (`mobile.md`), P6.
- **Sensitivity risk in C and A:** fruit pills, baby animals and celebrations shown to someone in TTC, IVF or after a loss. Mitigation: journey-state gating is a system requirement, not a content choice (C5).
- **Asset risk:** illustration and font rights (audit open questions). Mitigation: direction decisions here are about character, not specific files.
