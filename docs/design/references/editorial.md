# A. Premium editorial references

Looking for: strong typography, generous whitespace, editorial hierarchy, refined mobile typography, elegant long-form, restrained UI. Evaluated against the journal's title-plus-dotted-rule hierarchy, cream ground and ruled structure.

## A1. The Guardian (2018 redesign, web and app)

- **Source:** Design Week, It's Nice That, Creative Bloq coverage of the January 2018 redesign ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/15-21-january-2018/guardian-introduces-tabloid-format-redesigns-platforms), [It's Nice That](https://itsnicethat.com/news/theguardian-graphicdesign-150118), [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/the-guardian-launches-redesign-with-bespoke-type)).
- **Exact screen / interaction:** article page: Guardian Headline (Commercial Type) for headlines and standfirsts; the Guardian Egyptian text face retained with size and leading adjusted "with readability at its heart" (creative director Alex Breuer, as quoted).
- **Works:** one display family carries all authority; body type was *kept* and re-spaced rather than replaced; hierarchy via size and weight, not boxes. Mobile headlines remain large but short-measured.
- **Does not work for TSOY:** a news-density grid of many stories per screen; hard colour-coded section pillars (news/opinion/sport) would contradict P4 (one palette) if copied.
- **Translates:** the article page model: display headline, standfirst, a narrow text measure, and a bespoke-feeling display face over a conservative text face.
- **Must not copy:** Guardian typefaces, the pillar colour system, the masthead.
- **Principles:** supports P1, P2, P5; contradicts P4 if the pillar colours were borrowed.
- **Direction relevance:** B strong; A/C neutral.

## A2. Kinfolk (print and web, 2016 and 2021 redesigns)

- **Source:** It's Nice That on the 10th-anniversary redesign with Schick Toikka custom type ([It's Nice That 2021](https://itsnicethat.com/news/kinfolk-tenth-anniversary-redesign-schick-toikka-publication-graphic-design-230621)), Creative Bloq on the website ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/web-design/magazine-website-everyone-should-emulate-41514767)), magCulture interview with design director Alex Hunting ([magCulture](https://magculture.com/blogs/journal/alex-hunting-design-director-kinfolk)).
- **Exact screen:** feature opener spreads and the article page: serif text/deck/display family plus a secondary sans, images allowed to lead, long unbroken whitespace fields.
- **Works:** a **three-tier serif hierarchy** (display, deck, text) derived from one family gives richness without more fonts; whitespace is used as pacing (P5). Deliberate removal of ornament.
- **Does not work:** the aesthetic can read as performance (Desirability Lab critique, [link](https://desirabilitylab.com/backfill/backfill-2023-412-kinfolk-magazine-aesthetic)): cold, lifestyle-staged, and the photography register (muted, detached figures) is wrong for a health product that must feel warm and trustworthy (P9).
- **Translates:** deck/standfirst tier under the display title; images as full-width "spreads" between text blocks on the homepage and hubs; whitespace as chapter pacing (P7).
- **Must not copy:** the detached photographic coolness; the near-monochrome palette.
- **Principles:** supports P1, P5, P7; risks P9 (warmth).
- **Direction relevance:** B strong; cautionary for B's "cold drift" risk.

## A3. The New York Times (article typography; third-party reconstructions)

- **Source:** third-party type breakdowns ([webdesignhot DESIGN.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/nytimes/), [madegooddesigns](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=8989)), 2014 redesign review ([pxlnv](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/new-york-times-redesign/)). *No NYT documentation was found; sizes quoted by these sites disagree.*
- **Exact screen:** article page: NYT Cheltenham headlines, NYT Imperial body, NYT Franklin for labels and UI.
- **Works:** the **role split** (display serif / text serif / grotesque for labels and UI) is exactly the structure the journal already has (Adorn display / Source Serif letter / Aileron labels). A grotesque for navigation and UI keeps the serifs for content.
- **Does not work:** a light display weight was reported hard to read at small sizes (pxlnv); dense front page.
- **Translates:** the three-role type architecture; a sober grotesque for all UI chrome.
- **Must not copy:** the fonts, the news grid.
- **Principles:** supports P2 (two voices), P1.
- **Direction relevance:** B strong; A supportive (letter-style body serif).

## A4. Aesop (website; Work & Co)

- **Source:** third-party spec extraction ([webdesignhot Aesop](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/aesop/)), Font Alternatives note ([link](https://fontalternatives.com/inspiration/aesop-suisse/)), Footer Design ([link](https://www.footer.design/sites/aesop)).
- **Exact screen:** homepage and product page: Suisse Int'l grotesque on a warm ivory ground; reported h1 36 px at weight 400 and body near 12 px; light grey text token.
- **Works:** warm ivory rather than white (P3); hierarchy through restraint rather than scale; product-first layouts with no badges.
- **Does not work:** reported 12 px body and a #8a8a8a-class grey (AA for large text only) fail TSOY's accessibility bar; "declines to persuade" (Font Alternatives) and reads cold. This is the **cold-luxury** failure the audit warned about.
- **Translates:** the warm ivory ground and label-like quietness for packaging/product pages only.
- **Must not copy:** sub-16 px body, low-contrast greys, the emotional temperature.
- **Principles:** supports P3, P5; contradicts P9 and the accessibility requirement.
- **Direction relevance:** B cautionary; also listed in `anti-references.md` (cold luxury).

## A5. Practical Typography and GOV.UK typography scale (reading measure and size floor)

- **Source:** Butterick, *Practical Typography* on line length ([link](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html)); GOV.UK Design Notes on making the typography scale more accessible ([link](https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2022/12/12/making-the-gov-uk-frontend-typography-scale-more-accessible)); Smashing Magazine on measure vs size ([link](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2014/09/29/balancing-line-length-font-size-responsive-web-design/)).
- **Exact rule set:** 45–75 characters per line for single-column text; no body sizes of 14 px and below; mobile body not smaller than desktop.
- **Works:** gives TSOY measurable, defensible reading rules for articles and Companion answers.
- **Translates directly:** article measure 60–70 ch; body ≥ 16–17 px on mobile; Companion answer column with the same measure.
- **Principles:** supports P8 (room to read) and P9 (trust).
- **Direction relevance:** all; a constraint, not a style.

## Category conclusion

Editorial references confirm the **three-role typography** (display serif in spaced capitals for announcing; a text serif for reading; a neutral grotesque for prompts, labels and UI) and **whitespace as pacing**. They also show the failure mode: editorial restraint slides into coldness (Kinfolk, Aesop) unless warmth comes from somewhere. In TSOY that warmth source is the journal's blush/cream grounds, the prompt voice and, at chapter moments, the watercolour art.
