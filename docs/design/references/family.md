# D. Pregnancy / family references

Looking for: what works, what is clichéd, what is overly pink/feminine, what becomes childish, how sophisticated family brands create warmth. Evaluated against the journal's warm-but-restrained palette and its woodland watercolour art.

## D1. Flo (period and pregnancy app): the pink-and-pregnancy cliché

- **Source:** Polyester Zine critique ("pink as hell and obsessed with pregnancy") ([link](https://www.polyesterzine.com/features/the-worlds-most-popular-female-health-app-was-made-by-men-for-men)); Refinery29 on feminine app design ([link](https://refinery29.com/en-us/2014/02/63517/feminine-app-designs)); Broussard, *Technically Wrong* (user bothered more by the fertility assumption than the styling) ([Goodreads note](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/36125684-technically-wrong/145381-brian/cfbfbe4b-a731-4aaa-b7b7-da9df4107b8d)).
- **Exact pattern:** saturated pink UI; pregnancy pushed regardless of stated intent; heteronormative assumptions reported in reviews.
- **What fails:** colour as gender signal; assuming the user's goal; pregnancy content served to people who did not ask for it (which for TTC/IVF/loss users is harmful).
- **Translates (as avoidance):** TSOY's blush is a paper tone paired with sage, dusty blue and brown (P4); intent is asked, never assumed; TTC, IVF and loss states get their own content streams.
- **Principles:** contradicts P4 and P9.
- **Direction relevance:** guard-rail for all; C is most at risk of "pink and butterflies" drift because the journal cover has both.

## D2. What to Expect / BabyCenter (information portals)

- **Source:** comparison and review pages ([earnifyhub](https://www.earnifyhub.com/blog/babycenter-vs-what-to-expect), [Mequoda 2005 review](https://www.mequoda.com/articles/website-design/babycentercom-website-design-review)). *Low-authority and dated; used only to name the pattern, not as a measured audit.*
- **Exact pattern:** week-by-week pages surrounded by ads, community modules, sponsored product blocks; volume reported as "overwhelming".
- **What fails:** density and advertising dilute health trust; the week page is a feed, not a page (contradicts P1, P8).
- **What works:** the week-by-week spine itself is what users want and what the physical journal already has.
- **Translates:** TSOY keeps the week spine but renders it as a single calm page with the journal's week template; no ad-shaped modules; commerce separated (see `health-wellness.md` C6).
- **Direction relevance:** anti-model for B's hubs; see `anti-references.md`.

## D3. Bugaboo (premium family brand, website)

- **Source:** ecomm.design teardown ([link](https://ecomm.design/site/bugaboo)).
- **Exact pattern:** near-black and warm off-white base, colour applied sparingly, **a single sans-serif with weight for hierarchy**, campaign-led hero then a category grid.
- **What works:** restraint and one typeface prove a family brand can be premium without pastel; photography carries the warmth.
- **What does not:** product-catalogue structure; one-sans typography is less distinctive than TSOY's display serif.
- **Translates:** hierarchy by weight and size within a family; photography, not colour, as the warmth carrier on marketing surfaces.
- **Must not copy:** the black/white palette.
- **Principles:** supports P5; neutral on P3.
- **Direction relevance:** B.

## D4. Stokke (brand positioning) and Nuna (minimalist premium)

- **Source:** Nord Studio case for Stokke US (wanting to "stand apart from overly polished portrayals of parenthood") ([link](https://findyourquirk.com/work/stokke-built-for-growth)); Nuna brand description and Red Dot listing ([Lazada brand wiki](https://brandwiki.lazada.com.ph/nuna/), [Red Dot](https://www.red-dot.org/project/nuna-49414)).
- **Exact decision:** candid, less staged photography (Stokke); understated minimalism as the whole proposition (Nuna).
- **What works:** **candour** as a photographic direction: real homes, real light, not catalogue perfection; this is the antidote to generic pregnancy stock.
- **What does not:** without the journal's craft cues these brands read as furniture retail.
- **Translates:** TSOY photography brief: candid, natural light, real homes and real journals in use; no white-studio bump shots.
- **Principles:** supports P9 (honesty), P6.
- **Direction relevance:** B imagery.

## D5. Bobbie (infant formula) and Coterie (nappies): the "sophisticated DTC baby" register

- **Source:** logotyp.us on Bobbie's deep muted teal wordmark ([link](https://logotyp.us/logo/bobbie/)); Ypulse on Bobbie reinventing the category ([link](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2021/12/02/bobbie-is-rebranding-baby-formula/)); MediaPost on Coterie's "clean wipe aesthetic" ([link](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/398928/coterie-goes-soft-with-new-baby-wipe-marketing.html)).
- **Exact pattern:** one deep, muted brand colour instead of primary pastels; minimal packaging; adult-directed voice.
- **What works:** confirms that parents respond to being addressed as adults; a muted signature colour (TSOY's dusty blue-green #96ACB1 plays this role).
- **What does not:** these brands have no keepsake warmth; they are hygiene products.
- **Translates:** the dusty blue-green as the single signature accent, used for titles and key actions, not pink.
- **Principles:** supports P4.
- **Direction relevance:** B.

## D6. Peanut (mothers' community app)

- **Source:** TechCrunch on community groups becoming the main use ([link](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/13/social-network-for-motherhood-peanut-raises-5m-expands-to-include-women-trying-to-conceive)); user reviews ([TrustBurn](https://trustburn.com/reviews/peanut-app)).
- **Exact pattern:** social-app conventions (feeds, matching, chat) with a warm but conventional UI.
- **What works:** proves the TTC → pregnancy → motherhood continuum in one product (TSOY's journey continuity).
- **What does not:** social-feed UI is the opposite of a private keepsake; chat reliability complaints.
- **Translates:** continuity of identity across stages; nothing visual.
- **Direction relevance:** neutral.

## D7. Headspace (illustration-led wellness brand)

- **Source:** Blush interview with brand illustrator Karen Hong ([link](https://blush.design/blog/post/headspace-mindfulness-app)); Politecnico di Milano thesis on the "Corporate Memphis" style's pervasiveness ([link](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/handle/10589/201981)).
- **Exact pattern:** rounded, closed-eyed characters that are **functional** (they teach postures and visualise breathing) and appear across most screens.
- **What works:** illustration earns its place when it does a job; a consistent character system builds recognition.
- **What does not:** the style has become generic ("illustration everywhere"); constant characters lower perceived seriousness, which matters for health claims.
- **Translates:** TSOY's woodland characters must likewise *do a job* (mark a chapter, celebrate a milestone, soften an empty state) and otherwise stay off the page (P6).
- **Must not copy:** the flat-vector style; ubiquity.
- **Principles:** supports P6 when restrained; the failure mode is the anti-reference "illustration everywhere".
- **Direction relevance:** the key calibration for C.

## Category conclusion

Family references show that warmth comes from **voice, candour and craft**, not pink or cartoon. The journal's palette is already closer to Bobbie/Bugaboo restraint than to Flo. The woodland art is TSOY's distinctive asset, and Headspace shows both its power (recognition) and its danger (ubiquity). Loss-aware and intent-aware behaviour (D1, C5) matters more to this audience than any visual choice.
