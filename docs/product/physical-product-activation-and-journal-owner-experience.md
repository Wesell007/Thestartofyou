## 1. Core principle

**ACQUISITION SOURCE ≠ PRODUCT OWNERSHIP ≠ ENTITLEMENT ≠ JOURNEY.**

These are four separate facts, and must never collapse into one flag such as `came_from_journal = true`.

| Fact | Question it answers | Changes over time? |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition source | How did this person first or most recently arrive? | first touch is fixed; later touches are appended |
| Product ownership | Which physical product instances has this account verifiably claimed? | grows as products are claimed |
| Entitlement | Which owner-specific benefits are enabled? | granted and revoked independently of ownership |
| Journey | Which pregnancy, child or stage is a product connected to? | follows the 41B family/journey model |

The model must support all of these without resetting an account or a journey:
- someone who discovers TSOY through social media, search or the website and buys a journal later;
- someone who buys on Amazon and scans the insert card;
- someone who receives the journal as a gift;
- someone who already has an account before claiming;
- someone who owns several physical products;
- someone who owns journals for more than one pregnancy.

## 2. Ownership levels

| Level | Meaning | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| 0. No relationship | nothing known about a physical product | — |
| 1. Journal visitor / generic scanner | arrived from a printed QR or URL that is not unique | **none**: a self-declaration or generic marketing QR is never ownership |
| 2. Verified owner | the account successfully claimed a unique product instance | activation claim |
| 3. Linked to a journey | the claimed instance is connected to a specific pregnancy, child or journey | owner's explicit choice |

**Current state (Phase 27F):**
- `/journal-start` and `src/lib/journalOwner.ts` store a **device-level tone hint** in `localStorage`.
- The code states it is "never proof of purchase and never gates access".
- It is level 1 at most. It must not be migrated or treated as verified ownership. When verified ownership exists, the hint becomes a presentation fallback only, or is retired.

## 3. Journal owner entry experience (concept)

```
Physical journal → insert card → unique QR / activation code → TSOY activation URL
  → sign in or create account → claim the product instance → connect it to the relevant pregnancy journey
  → Journal Owner Experience ("Your journal continues here.")
```

A physical journal owner must not land as an unknown web visitor. Gate 2 explores:
- the journal-owner welcome;
- account creation after a scan;
- claiming with an existing account;
- connecting the journal to the current pregnancy;
- **"In your journal this week"**;
- the digital companion to physical prompts;
- photos, voice and memories alongside physical entries;
- relevant supplementary guidance;
- Companion awareness of ownership;
- **no repeated sales prompts** for a product already owned.

## 4. Digital-first users

Visitors from Google, Instagram, TikTok, direct web, editorial articles or the app go through normal TSOY onboarding. If they later buy and claim a journal:

```
digital-first user → claims journal → same account → ownership added → interface adapts → journal bridge appears
```

There is no new identity and no onboarding restart. Core health and support information is **never degraded** for people who don't own a journal. Physical ownership enhances the ecosystem; it is **not a paywall** for essential support.

## 5. Ownership verification model (not implemented)

- **One unique activation identifier per physical product instance.** A generic marketing QR is never verified ownership.
- **The token:**
  - is high entropy (generated by a CSPRNG, at least 128 bits, with a human-typable fallback code);
  - contains **no** e-mail, Amazon order id or other PII;
  - is stored server-side only as a **hash** (with a pepper or keyed hash), never as the raw claim code;
  - is printed codes are verified by hashing the submitted value.
- **Claim rules** (to be decided in design):
  - claimable once;
  - an already-claimed state for a second account, routed to support;
  - rate-limited verification attempts;
  - claim performed by a server function, never by direct client writes.
- **Recovery/support:** support can look up an instance by its printed serial or edition and the claim record, without needing or revealing the original token. Re-issuing transfers a claim through an audited path.

## 6. Two kinds of QR

| | A. Activation QR / code | B. Contextual journal QR |
|---|---|---|
| Where | insert card | anywhere in the journal (a week, topic or tool) |
| Unique per copy | **yes** | no (same in every copy of an edition) |
| Proves ownership | **yes**, once claimed | **no** |
| Destination | activation and claim flow | a week, topic, tool or content experience |
| When signed in | — | may adapt to existing ownership and journey context |

## 7. Future data model (concept only; NOT AUTHORISED)

| Candidate entity | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `products` | product catalogue: Pregnancy Journal, First Year product, milestone cards, future products |
| `product_instances` | an individual physical copy: edition/version, production batch where useful, activation identity (hash), printed serial |
| `product_claims` | which account claimed which instance; status, date, safe activation reference; transfer/support history |
| `journey_product_links` | which journey, pregnancy or child an instance belongs to, where applicable |
| `acquisition_attribution` | first touch, plus relevant later touches (journal insert, Amazon, Google, Instagram, TikTok, organic web), kept **separate** from ownership |
| `entitlements` | owner-specific benefits, deliberately separate from ownership |

Constraints for the later design:
- reconcile with the 41B family/journey architecture (`docs/strategy/phase41b0r-family-entity-architecture-reconciliation.md`) before any migration design (pregnancy episodes, babies, the journey pointer);
- RLS scoped to the owning account;
- service-only claim functions;
- account deletion (N10) must remove or anonymise claims, links and attribution under the D14 decisions;
- first applied on `tsoy-staging` only.

## 8. Amazon and gifts

The buyer may be the pregnant person, a partner, a relative or a gift-giver. The **activation claim** is the bridge to the person actually using TSOY. Amazon customer identity is **not** the ownership mechanism. A future Amazon attribution or integration may supplement acquisition analytics, but never replaces the claim.

## 9. Companion integration (planned)

Verified ownership becomes **permitted** Companion context:
- the product and edition;
- the linked journey;
- the relevant physical-week context.

For example: *"There's a reflection space for this stage in your journal if you'd like to capture this there."*

Rules:
- never recommend buying a product the user already owns;
- ownership context does **not** expose private journal entries, mental-health data or other sensitive data;
- the existing permission and memory architecture (`docs/ai/memory-design.md`, `companion-v1-expanded-roadmap.md`) still governs sensitive context;
- RED/CRISIS behaviour is unchanged.

## 10. App integration (planned)

The app inherits the same ownership model and backend. A future activation URL should open the web activation flow when the app is absent, and deep-link into the app when it is installed. The app Journal area connects:
- the **physical journal**: current week, reflection prompt, contextual scan;
- the **digital journal**: photos, video, voice, memories, milestones, Companion reflection support.

No native code now.

## 11. Analytics and commercial value (planned; consent-gated)

The model must later answer:
- acquisition source;
- activation rate;
- claim rate;
- account creation from scans;
- Companion adoption among owners;
- digital-journal usage;
- transition into First Year;
- repeat physical-product purchase;
- app adoption;
- retention across journey stages.

Rules:
- first-touch acquisition is never mixed with current ownership state;
- events follow the existing consent gate and `src/lib/analyticsEvents.ts` rules (no names, e-mails, dates, free text or health details);
- activation tokens are never sent to analytics;
- account deletion includes analytics identity erasure (M7).
